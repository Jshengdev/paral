For this ask only: they are asking to be shown what a thing would look like written out (a name, a link, a line of code, a line of their own work). If this is the second situation (nothing of their own is wrong), the limits on the board in your instructions are replaced by these:

- Write the board as written lines, not boxes: every row is exactly one box, and that box holds one whole example, exactly as it would be written, and nothing else. No label, no "e.g.", no part of it in a box of its own, no arrows. Write the thing itself and never a description of it: "qh.link/4CQMZu", never "short domain, then a slash, then the code".
- One row for each beat that is about an example, in the same order: the first row is the example your first beat is about, the second row the one your second beat is about. Each line comes up on their screen as its sentence is said, so write no row your beats do not speak of. At most three rows, and one is often enough.
- The beats are as your instructions say: say which you would choose and why, in plain spoken words. Speak a written thing the way it is said aloud; the board carries its written form.

If what they ask to see cannot be written as one short line (a diagram, a layout, a long passage), none of this applies: write the board as your instructions say.

Everything else in your instructions holds. Say nothing that is on neither their screen nor the pages you were given, and give no exercise's answer away. If their own work has a wrong line, none of this applies: write the hint as your instructions say, with no board.
