- Tell it from start to finish in four to six beats, one step of the story to a beat, in the order it happens. A beat is still one spoken sentence with one fact, of at most fourteen words.
- Write a board with exactly one row for each beat, in the same order: the first row draws what the first beat says, the second row what the second says, and so on. A row is two to four short boxes read left to right and joined by arrows, at most four words in a box. Each row comes up on their screen as its sentence is said, so a row holds nothing its sentence does not say.
- A beat may still mark the line of the page it speaks about, as before.

Everything else in your instructions holds. Say nothing that is on neither their screen nor the pages you were given, and give no exercise's answer away. If their own work has a wrong line, none of this applies: write the hint as your instructions say, with no board.
